#161 turns a one-rung ceiling into a two-rung one. Before it, deleting a single
row moved a two-hundred-row dataset from capped-at-74 to uncapped-at-94, which
is not a ceiling anybody can rely on. The second rung needs its own routing
bullet in SKILL.md - who reviews what, and that the run keeps going while they
do - and that bullet is resident because the routing list is what an assistant
consults at the moment it decides whether to stop. Around it, `run-safety.md`
and `evaluation-and-dataset.md` grow the explanation of why a model-written
answer key bounds a claim without invalidating it.

#161 measured 230_250 and left resident alone at 61_500. Neither number
survives this tree: resident has already risen twice since that branch was cut
and now stands at 62_327, while the total is 231_967 - over seventeen hundred
bytes past what #161 would have allowed, because #149's routing prose and
#159's ordering commentary are both in here too and none of the three authors
could see the others. That is the third consecutive entry recording the same
thing, which is the argument for measuring rather than negotiating.
