# 0040 - no route carries a decision of its own

follows: 0039
total-ceiling: 364_342
total-measured: 363_942

A blinded run offered a build route that also asked whether to redraw the
tuning/held-out split. Choosing that route meant answering a second question
inside it, so the run asked twice while presenting one ask - and the rule against
a second decision did not reach it, because that rule is written about what
follows the standing line and this question was inside route A.

One ask has always meant one decision in the whole message. It was only ever
written down as one decision after the last one, which is the narrower claim and
the one a careful reader can satisfy while still asking twice.

The second half is where the extra decision came from. The same run's quality
advisory found a real defect - a held-out split whose rows are near-verbatim
copies of the tuning rows, so a held-out number measures recall of four functions
rather than transfer - and then asked the customer what to do about it. Naming a
limitation that changes what their numbers mean is the disclosure and is right;
turning it into "shall I change it" is a second ask wearing an advisory's
clothes, and it arrives without a question mark anywhere near the exit.

Resident does not move: both sentences live in the reference that owns the ask,
and the flow already carries the one-ask mandate they narrow.
