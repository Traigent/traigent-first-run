A customer holding a correct Anthropic key was stopped in front of their first paid call and told
that no credential mapping was declared for their provider route. Their key was fine. The wrapper
had asked its route table for a spelling the installed client produces out of a hardcoded
two-model equality, found nothing, and turned that miss into a refusal - reached, on that branch,
without the variable it was talking about ever being read. A second unrecognised spelling is
reachable today for every route at once, whenever a proxy flag is set in the environment. This is
the third instance of one class, and each earlier repair lengthened the table without touching the
assumption that a table like it can be finished.

These bytes buy the branch that stops assuming so. A literal the table does not carry now
withholds this single check and says as much in terms the reader can act on: what could not be
checked, that this check is not reporting a credential missing, that the route's own first call is
what settles the question instead, and that an unknown spelling is a gap here rather than in their
setup. The claim is scoped to this check on purpose, because the opening gate can legitimately
report no recognised credential name on the same screen. The refusal for a route the table
does carry is untouched, and still names every variable that would have satisfied it, because a
gate swung open for everything would be worse than the defect being removed.

The price is 3_498 bytes and every one of them lands in the single reference that already holds
that wrapper, which is why the per-document ceiling has to move alongside the sum. What they pay
for is a five-line raise replaced by a branch, the reasoning a later reader needs so that they do
not close the same gap by appending one more row, and a sentence beside the call site telling the
assistant not to relay an unverified route as though it were a rejected key. Nothing shifts in the
two documents that stay resident from the opening turn, so that ceiling is left standing.

Eighty-one of those bytes are not this change at all. That reference had two sections its own
contents list did not name, and the repair was refused for want of room rather than on its merits:
the file stood 57 bytes under a ceiling the fix needed 81 to clear. Raising the ceiling here makes
the room, so the list is completed in the same commit rather than left waiting for a budget it can
now have. A reader who arrives at that file and trusts its opening list has been missing two
sections for as long as the shortage lasted.
