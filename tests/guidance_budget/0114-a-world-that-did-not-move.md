Eighteen hundred bytes for one arithmetic fact and the sentence a customer is owed while it is
unsettled. Every other bound in this section is about coverage - what a comparator can and cannot
tell apart - and this one is not: a checker that inspects what changed alters what the next
execution will find, and the guide runs the evaluator once per probe, once for the baseline, and
once per configuration per row. The first execution measures one thing and every later one measures
another, so two candidates are ranked partly on the order they happened to run in. It looks entirely
ordinary on the card, which is what makes it worth spending bytes on rather than leaving to be
noticed.

Nothing already written reaches it, and saying so is a third of the paragraph. The reserved split
answers a different question and its own rows are graded by the same moving ruler. Calibration's
separation checks compare four scores taken inside one probe set and cannot observe that the set
itself changed the world between them. The containment walk looks for an engine and a replay through
the project's own functions is not one. Three protections that a reader would reasonably assume
covered this, none of which does.

What is NOT decided here is deliberate and is stated so the omission cannot be mistaken for an
oversight. Requiring the check to be idempotent, requiring a reset between executions, and refusing
the shape outright are three different answers with three different costs, and choosing among them
is the owner's. What a customer is owed while nobody has chosen is the disclosure, and it goes in the
two places the sampled answer key already goes - before the run and at the close - because a reader
who meets it only once meets it in whichever place they were not reading.
