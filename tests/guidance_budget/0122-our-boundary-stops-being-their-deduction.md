Two kilobytes, and most of it is a glossary headword plus one disclosure bullet learning to stop
claiming something nobody checked. The entry before this one removed the block and left the number
alone. The number was the other half: a run whose evaluator executes the model's answers scored 45
out of 100, thirty-two points below what its own evidence earns, and every one of those points was
charged for a check THIS GUIDE declined to make. The corollary written at 0121 says deduct for what
the customer governs and never for a limit we chose, and the ceiling was that corollary's own
counterexample sitting three sections below it.

What replaces it was already built, which is why this costs so little. The evaluation pillar had
long since renormalized the refused check out of its own denominator and printed that it deducts
nothing; the band hold had long since refused the top two bands to any run whose weakest pillar
rests on too little. So the uncapped run reads 77 and bands WORKABLE, not STRONG, and the caveat is
structural rather than a sentence somebody has to remember to write. A ceiling was approximating
that, badly, in the one currency the customer feels.

It comes off one arm only, and the asymmetry is the whole safeguard. Where preflight walked the file
and PROVED the engine, the boundary is demonstrably ours and nothing is bounded. Where all this run
holds is the declaration flag, the ceiling stays at 45 - because removing it there would have paid
twenty-two points for typing a word about a file nobody read, and the rule against exactly that is
stated at the flag itself: a judgement may withhold a claim and may never manufacture one. Measured,
the declared arm now scores what a run that simply never calibrated scores, which is what its help
text always promised it did.

The glossary earns its entry by naming the thing the card now prints. A label reading NOT CHECKED
HERE is not self-explanatory, and it is the first label on that card that describes US rather than
the reader's project - every other one names something of theirs they could go and change. An
assistant asked what it means needs a prepared answer, and the answer has to include the part that
is easy to skip: it costs a claim rather than points.

And the disclosure stopped reassuring them. It said there is nothing for them to fix, which was
written to stop a customer being handed an errand and overshot into a statement of fact this run has
no standing for. We declined the check. Their query may be wrong, their column types may not match,
their cells may be null - nobody looked, so "nothing to fix" says we did. Its mirror image went with
it: "which may well be sound" is the same unearned verdict pointed the friendly way. What is left is
symmetric and true, that we did not check, that we therefore do not know either way, and that no
step in this guide would change it - not because they failed anything, but because this check is
outside what the guide performs.
