A hundred bytes, and most of them buy a placeholder. An earlier change removed accuracy as the
objective this run assumes and wrote the rule down plainly: the key is what the customer said they
were optimizing for, and a customer whose evaluator scores tool-use compliance optimizes tool-use
compliance. Six places went on naming the frontier after accuracy anyway, and one of them was not
guidance about a label - it was the label, rendered onto the customer's own result.

The safety reference is the clearest of them because it disagreed with itself inside one section.
Its body says twice that the axis is this run's own metric, the incumbent's score on it, whatever
the run declared. Its heading and the quoted sentence between them said accuracy. So the same
section told the assistant to read the axis off the run and handed it a sentence naming that axis
something else, and the sentence is the half a paying customer actually sees.

The flow document held the mandate rather than an example, which is why that one mattered more than
its length suggests. "Report each measurement as a Pareto frontier over accuracy and cost" is the
instruction an assistant follows; naming the declared objective there, in the customer's own words,
is what makes the other five consistent rather than merely reworded.

One sentence keeps the word and keeps it deliberately. Where a customer names no objective at all,
the guide proceeds on accuracy and cost and says so in those words - a default nobody chose must not
be mistaken for a choice somebody made, which is the whole reason that sentence exists. Renaming it
generically would have hidden the default behind a variable and lost the disclosure, so the only
occurrence left in the package is the one place the word is true by construction.

Also folded in, because it reads in the same pass and presupposes the same thing: the approval
paragraph spoke of an accuracy-only search and of proceeding at equal accuracy. Both are about a
quality axis against a cost axis and neither needed the word, so they name the objective score
instead and stop implying the run knows what it is.
