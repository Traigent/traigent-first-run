Eight hundred and thirty bytes, and the whole of it is one distinction the documents had collapsed.
`references/run-safety.md` said the evaluator check "is not made, so it earns nothing" - true of this
run and stated as though it were true of the world. traigent-first-run#506 is the finding that it was
not: a customer who measured their own evaluator on their own machine and handed the result to
`--calibration` was scored exactly as a customer who handed this run nothing, while a FAILING result
from that same source still convicted them. Same payload, same flag, opposite treatment, and the
recorded reason for it - "leniency bought by breaking the rule" - described a rule section 0 had
already withdrawn. This guide declines to PERFORM the check. It forbids the customer nothing.

So the passage now says which of the two it means, and the sentence that follows it is the one an
assistant can act on: where the project already holds its own result, pass it to `--calibration` and
leave `--calibration-scope-refused` off, because the two flags refuse each other and the second says
the opposite of the first. A scoring change nobody can reach from the guide is not delivered, and
`component-creation.md` is where an assistant decides what to pass.

What did NOT change is the disclosure, and the ledger should say why, because the obvious reading of
"the result is credited" is that the cap goes quiet. The cap is raised either way. The paid run still
has the customer's scorer execute statements the model wrote, against whatever it already reaches,
many times over - and nobody having calibrated anything touches that. What the result changes is the
arithmetic and three sentences of the cap that were false the moment the card counted it: this card
could not read the result, we do not know whether the evaluator works, no card can claim it grades
correctly. The rest of the reason is unchanged because the hazard is.

The provenance clause is new and it is SYMMETRIC, which is the part worth recording. This score never
runs a calibration; every payload it reads arrived from something it did not watch, and that was
already true in the ordinary arm and said nowhere. Naming it only where the shape is refused would
have marked the self-supplied result as the unverified one while an identically unverified payload
two arms over said nothing at all - which reads as though the ordinary one had been observed. The
clause is the same in both arms and on both verdicts, because the fact is.

And the objection the withdrawn reason was right about is recorded at the site rather than answered
away. The customer broke no rule; an assistant that ran this check from inside the guide did, and
this score cannot tell the two payloads apart, because both arrive as JSON on one flag. Crediting the
result therefore does pay for the forbidden route. The answer is that preventing it is not the
scorer's job: `calibrate_evaluator.py` refuses an evaluator that executes the candidate's answer,
naming the file and the line, so the tool this guide ships will not produce that payload at all, and
the guidance forbids taking the check another way. A scorer that withholds credit to enforce a rule
reports a lower number than it believes - which is the accusation 0130 has just finished removing
from this same cap.
