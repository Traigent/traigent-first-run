A hundred and forty bytes, and every one of them is a sentence that stopped being true when the
scorer changed underneath it. `references/component-creation.md` told an assistant that
`--calibration-scope-refused` "moves no number". It was true while the declared arm was charged for
the check it disclosed, and traigent-first-run#507 is the decision that it should not be: the
unmade check there is one THIS guide refused on the customer's behalf, and a run is never deducted
for our own boundary. The declaration now retires that charge, so the evaluation pillar moves - 31
to 59 on the fixture the suite pins - while the overall figure and the 45 ceiling stay exactly where
they were.

"Moves no number" was therefore about to become the compact, confident, wrong form of a true thing.
What survives is the part a reader of that paragraph actually needs before they pass the flag: it
lifts no ceiling and moves no readiness figure. What replaces the false half is the reason the
pillar does move, said as ownership rather than as arithmetic - a check this guide refused rather
than one this project skipped - because an assistant who knows only that a number went up has been
handed an incentive to pass the flag, and an assistant who knows why it went up has not.

not redo it. `references/evaluation-and-dataset.md` already speaks of `evaluator-calibration-refused`

untouched by a change that moves only which arm is charged. The pre-spend bullet in
`references/run-safety.md` is unconditional by construction and says nothing about deductions at all.

asserts both homes in one place precisely so neither can be corrected alone. It is not a guidance
document, so it costs this ledger nothing - but it is read by the assistant deciding whether to pass
a safety declaration, which is the reader who matters here. Both now say which numbers hold still
and why the pillar does not.
