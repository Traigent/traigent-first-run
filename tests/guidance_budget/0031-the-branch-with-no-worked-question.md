# 0031 - the branch with no worked question

follows: 0030
total-ceiling: 351_400
total-measured: 351_095

Two asks were written out as sentences a customer hears, and a third was left as
a rule. The reference showed what to say when material could not be found, and
what to say when there is too little of it, while the case where material exists
and cannot be used had only a line in the flow naming its three routes. A reader
reaches that branch, finds no words for it, and renders the words that are
there - which are the two answers belonging to a different situation. That is
what happened on a blinded run: correct diagnosis, correct stop, and a closing
question offering to go ahead or to hand over a path, with no repair anywhere in
it.

The branch deserves its own words because it owns a route the other one cannot
have. When material is absent there is nothing to mend, so the honest offer is
to write a substitute or be pointed elsewhere. When material is present and
broken, mending it is available, and it is the single route on which the run
still measures the customer's own product rather than a stand-in. Leaving that
route implicit costs the one outcome the whole exercise is for.

Most of what is bought is therefore the question itself, spoken rather than
described, plus the two rules that decide how it is laid out. The standing
`I have it` exit is not numbered beside the routes: it answers where material
lives, not what to do about the material already here, and numbering it turns an
always-available exit into one choice among four while pushing the pause into
another - which is how a set of three ways forward reads as a list of ways to
stop. And each route is stated by what it does, because a route named instead of
described drifts into its neighbour: "continue once a valid one is available" is
a pause wearing the substitute's number, and a run that offers it has offered
nobody a way to continue.
