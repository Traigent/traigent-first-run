RESIDENT falls by 224 and its ceiling comes down with it, while TOTAL rises by 1_270;
the shape of that split is the change itself. Two timings collapse into one, so the flow document stops carrying the instruction to
pick a subset at the pricing stage and carries a dispatch four sections earlier instead - a move
rather than an addition, and the paragraph it replaces was longer than the sentence that replaces it.
The reasoning all lands in the reference that owns the subset, where a reader meets it once.

Why the timing moves at all. A dataset this run generates reserves its held-out rows the moment its
working copy exists, before anything is designed around them, for a stated reason: nothing that
shapes a component may see those rows first. A dataset the customer brought did the opposite - the
evaluator was built while looking at the whole file, and ten of those rows were relabelled held-out
afterwards. Those rows had already shaped the ruler that was about to grade them. Drawing first and
designing second is strictly better blindness and it costs nothing, and it leaves one rule where
there were two.

What it costs is written down rather than argued away, because it is real. Rule 1 - score the
dataset, never the subset - used to be kept by arithmetic: no subset existed at any scoring gate, so
nobody could score one by mistake. It is now kept by a sentence, and this repository is right to
distrust that trade. The sentence is stated at the top of the six rules, the reason it is
load-bearing is stated beside it, and a test refuses an edit that drops either - which cannot make
anyone read it, and can stop it disappearing quietly, which is how a rule kept by prose actually
dies.

One boundary is stated because both readings pass: not at the opening card. That gate runs before
any creation or repair, so no duplicate has been dropped, no placeholder filled and no id repaired,
and a draw there would choose rows that the repair then changes or removes.
