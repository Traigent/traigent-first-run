One branch, #202. Almost every byte lands in SKILL.md because the close is flow
and flow has one home; the glossary takes the rest, three lines, because its
plain-language entry for the card enumerated when a re-score is taken and what
each is read for, and would otherwise have been left describing a reading that
no longer exists. The old recommendation was assembled by scoring the
project again once the walkthrough had finished writing into it, then dropping
whatever no longer complained. On the input this guide is built for - somebody
arriving with no examples at all - that drops the very thing they most need to
hear: after the run there is a file of rows on their disk, our rows, and the
condition that fired at the opening is quiet. Nothing in the post-run evidence
distinguishes rows a product produced from rows this walkthrough invented an
hour earlier, so the discard was never a judgement; it was a blind spot wearing
one.

The replacement costs more than the sentence it removes, and the extra is
argument rather than instruction. Ranking off the arrival state needs one clause
to say where the ranking comes from. Saying that a substitute leaves a gap open
needs a second, because "filled" and "closed" read alike to anybody skimming a
green report. And the move itself is two moves, not one: fetch genuine examples
and derive the grading rule out of what those examples are expected to answer,
or else keep what was manufactured and put a human in front of it - both the
rows and the grading rule, whose notion of a right answer has to line up with
what the agent is handed and what it is meant to return. Their sequence is
load-bearing, so it is stated as a sequence and pinned as one.

Two smaller pieces ride along. The bounds this pass chose for itself - how many
rows it scored, how many configurations it paid for, how many controls it moved -
are not shortcomings of anybody's project, and the close now says what widening
each would buy, phrased as something to go and do rather than as a promise about
what would turn up. And the post-run score keeps exactly one job, which is
spelled out where the call is made: it is the only moment in the walkthrough at
which the agent pillar is scored on anything, since both earlier gates refuse a
settings document on purpose. Deleting the call would have stranded the
freeze-remove-persist sequence that manufactures that document, for a ranking
nobody reads any more.
