Seven rules bought here, all of them found by watching somebody read what a
run had already written, and five of them are one rule arriving in five places. The
first is register. The intent gate said a choice must be the job and never a
category, and a run obeying that offered "pull the metric, the time range, and
the grouping out of a plain-English data request" to a customer whose entire
project was a single text note. Not one of those three is a category name, so
nothing refused them, and the reaction was to ask why a time range is worth
saying to a customer at all. Naming the job turns out not to be enough on its
own; the vocabulary has to be theirs too.

So the gate states a property instead of lengthening its list of banned category
names: a word belonging to the trade a task comes from, rather than to the
project sitting in front of this run, is the category wearing a job's clothes.
Seven such words are named so an author has something to apply, and it says what
to do where their files have no word for a thing - say the thing plainly -
because otherwise an author keeps the trade word and hedges it. Recommending
costs the rest of that spend. "Structured and deterministically scoreable" holds
before anybody opens the project, so a recommendation resting on it alone is
identical on every run and reads like it; the gate now quotes the sentence that
named what in the customer's own log lines picked the task, with the failing one
beside it. Both belong in the resident document, because the gate fires before
any reference loads and a rule arriving later arrives after the choices are
written.

The remaining four are the one about marking. The creation reference already
held that a pair with nothing marked is a menu, and that a menu offered instead
of a recommendation is no recommendation. It was written for the build route,
justified by continuing beating pausing, and applied nowhere else. That
justification has a blind spot: it sorts continuing from pausing and falls
silent when two offered routes each continue, which happened on a set of 300
rows resolving to six distinct questions, and the mark landed on the route that
disturbed nothing over the route that could deliver an answer. The added
paragraph supplies the property that settles it - among routes that carry on,
mark the one able to produce the result being paid for - written without naming
a pillar, since the next instance will not be a comparison set.

The same absence reached the spend approval, which was an unmarked pair in two
live runs and rendered bold words where every other ask renders lettered routes.
Both are fixed by pointing that reference's rule at it rather than by writing a
second one: proceeding carries the mark, on the reasoning already stated one
section over and stronger here because what is approved is deliberately small,
and the mark is withheld under a blocking cap or where the run as scoped cannot
answer what it is paid for. A single shape for named-route choices is stated
once in the resident document, with the standing exit and a lone proposed action
excluded by scope rather than by exemption.

A live run then showed the same absence one message later, in its worst form.
The connected preview closed on a single yes/no about continuing, on a card
that had already named stopping and priced it four ways - so the route it
argued hardest about was the one the customer had no way to take, and reading
it cold the reaction was that no options had been offered at all, not even what
to type to proceed. Worse than an unmarked pair, where both answers are at
least available. That preview now ends on lettered routes with a reply form on
each, rendered as their own block below the disclosure prose rather than as a
sentence inside its last paragraph, since a reply form buried among recipients,
data contracts and key scopes is not an instruction. The same run also inverted
a caution into a sale - a tie among the top configurations offered as room a
wider search could move - so the reference now says which findings may fill
that slot and why a tie is not one: resolution comes from rows, and adding
candidates gives nothing more to tell them apart with.

Last, the baseline checkpoint, where the guidance's word order became the
output's emphasis order. Told to say stopping is valid and then to recommend
continuing, a run bolded the stop and trailed the recommendation. The
recommendation now leads, reframed as what continuing produces rather than what
stopping costs, and detached from the headroom clause that made it disappear on
a nearly perfect baseline - two of its reasons hold on any sound one, since
managed selection against their own agent and a generalisation check on their
own rows are neither of them numbers. Declining is kept and pinned, because the
next phase spends more and an approval nobody can decline is the worse defect,
and three limits are written down so a stronger case cannot become a promise of
improvement.

The sentence "this checkpoint is a valid place to stop" was described above as
reordered. It was deleted. 0069 had added four facts to it precisely because the
earlier text was true and incomplete, so removing the half 0069 called true,
silently, is the ledger claiming a move that did not happen. A positive
statement is restored below the recommendation instead - stopping leaves a real
result, a measured baseline on their own material - and the emphasis defect
stays fixed because the sentence now sits under the lead rather than above it.
The paragraph that mandated the gain frame also opened on a loss frame of its
own ("has already paid for the baseline and seen none of what the product does
with it"), which is one of the three limits it writes down, violated one
sentence earlier; it is gone.

Four evidenced branches contradicted the unconditional lead - a
non-discriminating pair, a nearly perfect baseline, a baseline with no measured
headroom, and a binding tie - and by this entry's own stated mechanism, that
word order is what a run reproduces, the lead won. The checks now run above the
lead, and the lead says what they can move: which continuing route carries the
mark, never a recommendation to stop. That keeps continuing the default
everywhere else and needs no headroom clause to say it.

Withholding the mark is replaced by moving it. The approval said the mark is
withheld under a blocking cap while the resident rule said exactly one route
carries it, and nothing compared the two, so the suite was green on a
contradiction; worse, withholding mandates an unmarked pair in the three states
where an invented one does the most damage, which the creation reference
records a blinded run supplying for itself. Under a blocking cap the route that
can produce what is being paid for is the fix, so the fix carries the mark. The
condition is written once and pointed at twice - the connected preview restated
all three conditions verbatim, and that duplicate is what let a deletion from
the card itself pass every guard over it.
