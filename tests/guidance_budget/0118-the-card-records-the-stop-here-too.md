71 bytes, and they close the half of a decision that landed in one document and
not in the other. When the evaluator-execution scope gate began to block rather than to bound, the
flow document's routing paragraph was corrected to say so, on the argument that a ceiling whose entry
omits the one way it differs from the neighbour it shares a number with is worse than no entry. The
safety reference describes the same card two sections after stating the stop itself, and there it
still named only the ceiling.

The document was never contradictory, which is why nothing failed and why this took a read of the
whole thing to notice. The stop is stated plainly forty lines earlier - the guide ends before
calibration, credentials, provider calls and paid work - so a reader who arrives at the top of the
section is told. A reader who arrives at the paragraph about the customer's card is told the number
and not the stop, and that is the paragraph somebody quotes when they are explaining the card to the
person holding it.

Small enough to be worth naming as a class rather than as an edit. A decision that changes what a
condition DOES has to be carried to every place that describes what it does, and the places are
rarely adjacent: one is a flow mandate and one is an explanation written for a customer who did
nothing wrong. The first was found because the change was being made there; the second was found by
reading the document end to end, which is the review this repository asks for and the one that is
easiest to skip when the diff looks small.
