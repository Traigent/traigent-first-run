#211, stacked on #203. Until now a project arriving without examples got them
written for it quietly, and heard about the substitution later, as a ceiling
printed beside a number it had already shaped. Nobody was lied to and nobody was
consulted either, which is a strange thing for the front door of a product to
do. The owner's flow puts the sentence where it belongs: at the moment of
discovery, while nothing has been created and nothing has been billed, phrased
as a choice with two exits rather than a notice with one.

The expensive property is that it is a single question. Three components can be
absent and a guide that mentions each in turn has technically disclosed
everything while turning a greeting into paperwork - so the flow file spends its
bytes on that constraint and on the four things the question must carry, and
nothing else. Two of those four are the honest half, and they are why this is
not merely a prompt for permission: what gets written is weaker than what the
customer would have brought, it holds the number out of the upper bands however
strong the remaining pillars are, and nothing from such a run may be pushed into
production. The remaining bytes buy the second exit, which is what makes the
first one voluntary - a pointer to material they already own.

Most of the addition is therefore in the creation reference, not in the flow: the
sentence as a reader would actually hear it, and the three ways a pointer fails
to resolve. A location nobody can open, a location that opens and yields nothing
readable, and a location supplied for one absence out of two. Each needed a
written destination, because every one of them otherwise terminates in the same
place - repeating the question - and repeating it is precisely the failure the
single-question rule was written against. The middle case earns its lines twice
over: it is where an agreement about absence must stop applying, since agreeing
that something is missing has never been agreeing to be graded by something
defective.
