A hundred and sixty bytes, all of them in the flow document, and what they buy is one sentence that
keeps two documents from answering the same question differently. The safety reference already ends
the run where an evaluator opens its engine against the customer's own database: no calibration, no
credentials, no provider call, nothing paid. The card meanwhile answered OK and told the reader the
paid run could begin. Both were shipped, both were followed, and a customer arriving at that state by
doing exactly what they were told met two artifacts disagreeing about whether their run was over.

RESIDENT is where the cost lands because that is where the routing paragraph lives, and it has to.
The paragraph is a list of what each ceiling means for the run, read once per condition by whoever is
deciding what to say next, and a ceiling whose entry omits the one way it differs from its neighbour
is worse than no entry: the two conditions share a number, share a pillar and sit in consecutive
sentences, so a reader who is not told they differ will assume they do not.

Why the words are so few for a change this size. Everything else the decision moves is machinery - a
route category, a blocking flag, an ask that a blocking condition no longer needs - and none of it is
guidance. The reference that ends the run needed no edit at all, because it was already right; the
correction runs the other way, from the document into the code, which is the direction that costs
almost nothing here and is also the direction that says which of the two was authoritative.
