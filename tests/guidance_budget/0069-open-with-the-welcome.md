Two live runs of the same guide disagreed about when to greet the user, and the
document is why. "Open with the five-stage journey" sat as one bullet among a
dozen presentation rules, ninety lines above `### 1. Inspect quietly`, whose own
text said only to begin read-only discovery. An assistant looking up what to do
first landed on the step, inspected, scored, and named the five stages for the
first time when it stopped to ask for a provider key. Both readings are honest;
the page is what was ambiguous. Moving the instruction into the step that
executes it costs bytes and removes the ambiguity that produced two behaviours.

What was missing was placement, not content: several checks already read the
greeting's words, and none of them asked where it is printed. This raise ships
with the check that closes that, asserted inside section one's own slice, so a
future edit that returns the instruction to the rules list fails rather than
merely changing a byte total.

The second half buys vocabulary rather than numbering. Progress lines print
`Stage N/5` while this document numbers its own sections one to eight, and a run
standing in a later section truthfully reported its next move as "isolated SDK
environment and free mock plumbing" - words no customer had been given. An
earlier draft answered that with a section-to-stage map, which was wrong at one
row and collided with twenty-nine places that already use the word to mean a
section. Naming the five stages is the smaller and correct instruction.

Two more sentences earn their bytes at the payment boundary. The baseline
checkpoint was described only as a valid place to stop, which is true and
incomplete: a user who stops there has never seen Traigent pick a trial, has no
portal experiment, no recommendation across both runs, and no held-out score,
and could not weigh a choice nobody had priced for them. And the pre-key
disclosure said the linked pages do not establish training use, then stopped -
leaving a reader to assume the worst about material the pinned telemetry
contract says is never transmitted. Stating what actually crosses answers the
question they are really asking.
