Five rules governed the bounded subset and none of them said the drawn rows
have to be different. On a file holding three hundred rows that resolve to six
questions, or forty whole-row copies among a few hundred originals, a compliant
draw could put the same question in the eighteen several times over. The run
plan records eighteen ids, the money approval prices eighteen, and twelve
trials buy two hundred and sixteen provider calls to observe far fewer outcomes
than that. Nothing in the package objected, because every existing rule was
about where the rows come from - which split, which band, recorded how - and
none was about whether any two of them are the same row.

What these bytes buy is the missing property, at the one place it can be
enforced without a judgement call. Selection can insist on exact equality of
input and expected answer, and exact equality has no false positive: two
byte-identical rows are one row under every reading anybody could bring. The
scoring path cannot use that strictness, because a customer who reworded their
questions defeats it, and the looser measure that would catch them was measured
against an instruction-prefixed support corpus and reported four comparable
examples where a hundred and twenty different tickets sat. So the strict test
goes where strictness is enough and nothing is refused on a guess.

The second paragraph exists because two constraints now meet and the older one
already carries a warning. Keeping four rows per band is what protects the
spread, and a trim that loses a band prints a complaint about data that has all
four - so an author de-duplicating without further instruction had a live way to
cause exactly the harm the paragraph beneath warns them off. The resolution is
stated rather than left to be worked out: thinning is allowed, emptying is not,
a band down to its single distinct row stays in the draw, and the shortfall is
named beside the sample-size limitation the run report already carries.

The resident line moves too, and it is four words plus a clause. Stage 6 named
eighteen and priced "that subset", which was unambiguous only while the two
were always equal. They can now differ downward, so the flow says which of them
the estimate is built from - the rows actually drawn - and says that the draw
can come in under the default. A pricing sentence that names a number the run
may not use is the kind of quiet disagreement between two documents this
package has already had four of.
