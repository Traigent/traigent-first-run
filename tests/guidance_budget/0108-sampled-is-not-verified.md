One budget moves. TOTAL goes up by 1_386, which is about one median raise as this directory measures
its own history, and RESIDENT stays inside the ceiling 0107 left standing - 105_593 under an
unchanged 105_600. That split is the whole shape of what this change was allowed to cost. RESIDENT
is loaded on the first turn and carried to the last, so a byte there is paid on every turn of every
run; a byte in a reference is paid once, by whoever followed a pointer to it. An earlier revision of
this branch spent 685 of the expensive kind and was refused for it, correctly.

What the bytes buy is a hold that a run can actually lift. The top two bands were held until a read
covered every row the search is graded against, and on any corpus above the drawn subset no read
ever could: the review names the rows that were drawn while the score reads the declared split, so
on this guide's own 4,812-row worked example the customer did everything asked of them and the card
still reported that nobody had looked. Worse, the reference promised a release at the section-4
re-score which the predicate refused, so the guidance and the code disagreed about a state the
customer arrives in by following the guidance.

The repair replaces the population rather than shrinking the threshold. What the search is graded on
is not the file - it is the bounded draw taken out of it, eighteen tuning rows and the held-out ten
- and that is small enough to read whole. So where the split is settled the read covers all of it,
and the released hold then carries an exact claim about the comparison instead of a probabilistic
one about the corpus. Where nothing has been drawn yet there is no such population to cover, and
five rows of what the customer brought is what is asked for and what the card calls it.

The cost of that decision is the word a customer takes away, so the bytes go where that word is
settled. `references/evaluation-and-dataset.md` says which rows are read at which moment, why a full
comparison of a large file stays refused during onboarding, and what a released hold does and does
not establish. Beside it, the disclosure that where this run wrote the dataset or the method, the
read was taken through its own work. The glossary entry defining the held band described a
row-by-row read of everything, which this guide no longer performs, and had to stop.
